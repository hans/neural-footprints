Spec: Exploratory Residual Analysis Module for Neural Representation Simulation Project
=======================================================================================

Objective
---------

Extend the existing simulation framework with an **exploratory module** investigating whether residualization / confound regression provides a reliable solution to the statistical asymmetry between high-dimensional sensory variables and low-dimensional abstract representations.

Rather than presupposing a specific failure mode, this module should systematically test when residual analyses produce:

*   accurate recovery of abstract variables,
    
*   false negatives (removal of genuine abstract signal),
    
*   false positives (apparent abstract signal driven by remaining sensory structure),
    
*   unstable or specification-dependent conclusions.
    

The aim is to determine whether residualization behaves robustly in realistic generative settings, or whether it inherits the broader limitations of variance-based methods.

Scientific Motivation
---------------------

A common response to sensory confounds in neuroscience is to regress out low-level stimulus features and then test whether residual neural activity still contains high-level information.

This strategy is often treated as a methodological safeguard, but its reliability depends on assumptions that may not hold in perceptual systems:

*   sensory and abstract variables are separable,
    
*   nuisance predictors are adequately measured,
    
*   relationships are approximately linear,
    
*   residual variance preserves meaningful representational content,
    
*   inference from sensory evidence does not itself create dependence.
    

Because natural sensory inputs are often generated by latent causes, high- and low-level variables may be deeply entangled. The present module should examine the consequences empirically within transparent synthetic systems.

Core Research Questions
-----------------------

1\. Can residualization recover genuine abstract variables?
-----------------------------------------------------------

Under what statistical regimes does removing sensory predictors preserve access to known high-level latent structure?

2\. When does residualization suppress true signal?
---------------------------------------------------

Does covariance between sensory features and latent causes lead to loss of real abstract information?

3\. When does residualization create spurious evidence?
-------------------------------------------------------

Can omitted nuisance dimensions or partial controls leave residual patterns that mimic abstraction?

4\. How sensitive are conclusions to model assumptions?
-------------------------------------------------------

Do results depend strongly on dimensionality, feature completeness, linearity, or noise?

Experimental Design
-------------------

Use the same synthetic framework as the existing project:

1.  Generate latent abstract variables.
    
2.  Generate sensory features with controllable dependence on latent structure.
    
3.  Generate neural responses from known combinations of these variables along with the inverse model activations.
    
4.  Apply residualization strategies.
    
5.  Evaluate what can be recovered from residual signals.
    

Ground truth should always be known, enabling direct comparison between inference and reality.

Generative Regimes to Explore
-----------------------------

### Independent Regime

Sensory and abstract variables partially separable.

### Correlated Regime

Sensory features statistically shaped by latent causes.

### Omitted Variable Regime

Only a subset of sensory predictors available for residualization.

### Nonlinear Regime

Interaction terms, mixed selectivity, gain modulation, or manifold structure.

### Dimensionality Imbalance Regime

High-dimensional sensory predictors with compact latent structure.

Analysis Outputs
----------------

Measure outcomes using simple interpretable metrics such as:

*   decoding accuracy of latent variables,
    
*   variance explained,
    
*   representational similarity recovery,
    
*   stability across specifications,
    
*   comparison before vs after residualization.
    

Figure Targets
--------------

### Panel A

Residual performance across regimes.

### Panel B

Example settings where residualization improves inference.

### Panel C

Example settings where residualization obscures or fabricates inference.

### Panel D

Sensitivity to nuisance model misspecification.

Interpretation Constraints
--------------------------

The purpose is not to argue that residualization always fails, nor that it always succeeds.

Instead, determine whether it is:

*   robust and trustworthy across plausible regimes, or
    
*   fragile and contingent on assumptions rarely guaranteed in practice.
    

This keeps the project scientifically credible and discovery-oriented.

Success Criteria
----------------

The extension succeeds if it reveals a clear empirical pattern about residual methods in synthetic neural systems, such as:

*   broad robustness,
    
*   systematic failure modes, or
    
*   sharp dependence on generative assumptions.
    

Any of these outcomes would be informative and would sharpen the paper’s central argument about the limits of variance-based inference.
